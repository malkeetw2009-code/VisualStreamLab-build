DMCA Takedown Policy

Effective date: November 22, 2026

This page describes a reporting process; it does not mean VisualStream Lab hosts or controls every stream, link, or item displayed in the Service. Complete the designated-agent details below before relying on this page as a formal DMCA notice address.

1. Reporting alleged infringement

VisualStream Lab respects intellectual-property rights and expects users and content providers to do the same. If you believe material accessible through the Service infringes a copyright you own or are authorized to represent, send a written notice to the copyright contact below. Identify the specific page, URL, channel, or item so we can locate it. A general complaint without enough detail may delay review.

2. What a DMCA notice must include

For a notice under the United States Digital Millennium Copyright Act (17 U.S.C. § 512), include all information required by law, ordinarily:

  1. A physical or electronic signature of the copyright owner or a person authorized to act for the owner.
  2. Identification of the copyrighted work claimed to have been infringed. If multiple works are covered, provide a representative list.
  3. Identification of the allegedly infringing material and information reasonably sufficient to locate it, such as exact URLs or channel/item identifiers.
  4. Your name, mailing address, telephone number, and email address, if available.
  5. A statement that you have a good-faith belief that the disputed use is not authorized by the copyright owner, its agent, or the law.
  6. A statement that the information in the notice is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that you are the owner or authorized to act for the owner.

4. Review and action

After receiving a sufficiently detailed notice, we may review the report, remove or disable access to the identified material where we control it, forward the notice to the person who submitted the material when appropriate, or refer you to the third-party host or source when the material is not hosted or controlled by us. We may request additional information. We do not guarantee a particular outcome or response time except where law requires one.

We may restrict accounts or remove material where we reasonably believe there is repeated infringement or other unlawful use. We will consider relevant counter-notices and applicable law before taking further action.

5. Counter-notices

If material you submitted was removed or disabled because of a DMCA notice and you believe the removal resulted from a mistake or misidentification, you may send a counter-notice to the designated agent above. A counter-notice under 17 U.S.C. § 512(g)(3) must include:

  1. Your physical or electronic signature.
  2. Identification of the material removed or disabled and its location before removal.
  3. A statement under penalty of perjury that you have a good-faith belief the material was removed or disabled because of mistake or misidentification.
  4. Your name, address, and telephone number, and a statement that you consent to the jurisdiction of the appropriate U.S. federal district court for your address—or, if outside the United States, a district where the Service provider may be found—and that you will accept service of process from the notice sender or its agent.

Where the DMCA procedure applies and all statutory conditions are met, we may forward a counter-notice to the original complainant. We may restore access between 10 and 14 business days after forwarding it unless the designated agent receives notice that the complainant has filed an action seeking a court order to restrain the allegedly infringing activity, as described in the statute.

6. Third-party content and other jurisdictions

Some content may be linked from, streamed by, or embedded from third-party services. If VisualStream Lab does not host or control the source, contact the relevant source or hosting provider as well. This policy does not limit rights or remedies available under laws outside the United States. We may respond to notices under other applicable copyright laws.

If the operator intends to rely on the DMCA safe-harbor procedures, it should confirm eligibility, designate and maintain an agent as required by law, and register the agent with the U.S. Copyright Office where required.
